## Main figures

**Figure 1. Study design and measurement contexts.**
Schematic of the three testing contexts. In the sequential battery (BT1; n = 136), each fish underwent the light-dark test (LDT), then the novel tank test (NTT), then the swimming-resistance assay. In the alternative, reversed behavioural context (BT2; n = 139), fish underwent the NTT followed by the LDT, without the swimming-resistance assay. In the isolated endurance context (BT3; n = 135), fish underwent the swimming-resistance assay alone. Ethanol (0, 0.5, or 1%) was applied for 1, 24, or 96 h; it remained present during the LDT and NTT and was absent from the swimming-resistance assay, which was conducted in culture water. Light-dark and novel tank endpoints were compared between BT1 and BT2, and endurance endpoints between BT1 and BT3, within matched treatment x exposure cells.

**Figure 2. Primary battery-context comparisons.**
Observed distributions of representative endpoints in the sequential battery and the corresponding isolated context, by ethanol concentration (CTR, 0.5%, 1%) and exposure duration (1, 24, 96 h). (A) Time in the bright zone (LDT), BT1 versus BT2. (B) Total distance (NTT), BT1 versus BT2. (C) Swimming-resistance index, BT1 versus BT3. Boxplots show medians and interquartile ranges with individual fish overlaid; the panels display distributions, not effect-size estimates. Between-context differences were tested with Mann-Whitney tests within matched treatment x exposure cells and adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure. Effect sizes (Hedges' g, rank-biserial correlation) and bootstrap confidence intervals are reported in Table 3. Cell sample sizes are given in Table 1.

**Figure 3. Fifteen-endpoint Spearman correlation heatmap.**
Pairwise-complete Spearman rank correlations among all 15 registered BT1 endpoints, including blood glucose and condition factor (pairwise n = 110-136; pairwise sample sizes in Supplementary Table [Sx]). Cell colour encodes the correlation coefficient (rho); a diagonal slash marks pairs with an unadjusted p >= 0.05. Endpoints are grouped by assay domain. The analysis is exploratory and descriptive; strongly correlated endpoints (for example, the swimming-resistance index and the final flow step) are not independent measures.

**Figure 4. Fourteen-endpoint PC1-PC2 PCA biplot.**
Exploratory principal component analysis of 14 standardized BT1 endpoints (blood glucose excluded), computed on complete cases (n = 135 of 136) after centring and scaling, with no imputation. (A) PC1-PC2 score-loading biplot; points are individual fish coloured by ethanol concentration and shaped by exposure duration for orientation only, with all 14 loading vectors shown. (B) Enlarged PC1-PC2 loading view. PC1 explained 23.3% and PC2 explained 17.2% of the variance (cumulative 40.6%). Component signs are arbitrary. The analysis was used only for exploratory visualization and not to test ethanol or measurement-context effects; no group separation is inferred. The complete variance spectrum is shown in Figure S1.

**Figure 5. BT1 light-dark test ethanol endpoints.**
Light-dark endpoints in the sequential battery (BT1; n = 136) by ethanol concentration (CTR, 0.5%, 1%) and exposure duration (1, 24, 96 h). (A) Time in the bright zone. (B) Mean time per crossing. Boxplots show medians and interquartile ranges with individual fish overlaid. Endpoints were analysed with aligned-rank-transform ANOVA, for which inference is rank-based; letters denote Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted pairwise contrasts, and groups sharing a letter do not differ. Model terms are reported in Table 4 and contrasts in Table 5.

**Figure 6. BT1 novel tank ethanol endpoints.**
Novel tank endpoints in BT1 (n = 136) by concentration and exposure. (A) Total distance. (B) Upper-stratum preference. Boxplots as in Figure 5. Endpoints were analysed with aligned-rank-transform ANOVA. Total distance showed a treatment x exposure interaction, and letters denote Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted contrasts. Upper-stratum preference showed a significant omnibus treatment term but no significant pairwise treatment contrast; no localized group difference is implied. Model terms are reported in Table 4.

**Figure 7. BT1 swimming-resistance-index ethanol endpoint.**
Swimming-resistance index in BT1 (n = 136) by concentration and exposure. Boxplots as in Figure 5. The endpoint was analysed with aligned-rank-transform ANOVA; treatment and exposure terms are reported in Table 4, and letters denote Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted contrasts. The related ordinal final-flow-step endpoint (proportional-odds model, Table 4) is not plotted here and is not an independent confirmation of the resistance-index result.

**Figure 8. BT1 blood-glucose endpoint.**
Blood glucose (mg/dL) in BT1 (n = 111 fish with a glucose value) by concentration and exposure. Boxplots show medians and interquartile ranges with individual fish overlaid. The endpoint was analysed with aligned-rank-transform ANOVA; the exposure and treatment x exposure terms were significant (Table 4). Letters denote Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted contrasts derived on the aligned-rank scale, while plotted values are in mg/dL. At 1 h no pairwise difference was detected. At 24 h the 0.5% and 1% groups differed, with the control intermediate and the 1% group highest. At 96 h the 0.5% and 1% groups differed, with the control intermediate but the 0.5% group highest and the 1% group lowest, reversing the direction seen at 24 h.

## Supplementary figures

**Figure S1. Complete PCA variance spectrum.**
Scree plot of the exploratory 14-endpoint BT1 principal component analysis (n = 135), showing the percentage of variance explained by each component and the cumulative variance. Components beyond PC2 are shown as variance values only and are not biologically interpreted; interpretation in the main text is limited to PC1 and PC2 (Figure 4).

**Figure S2. Light-dark first choice and latency (BT1).**
BT1 (n = 136) by concentration and exposure. (A) First-choice zone (light or dark), shown as stacked proportions; analysed with Firth penalised logistic regression, with significant coefficient-level treatment x exposure terms at 24 h and 96 h (Table 4). (B) Latency to first entry; analysed with a two-part hurdle model that was non-significant in both components, which is the governing latency result. Boxplots show medians and interquartile ranges with individual fish overlaid.

**Figure S3. Light-dark zone transitions (BT1).**
Number of zone transitions in BT1 (n = 136) by concentration and exposure. Analysed with a negative-binomial generalised linear model; no term was significant (Table 4).

**Figure S4. Novel tank mean velocity (BT1).**
Mean velocity in BT1 (n = 136) by concentration and exposure. Analysed with aligned-rank-transform ANOVA; the endpoint showed a treatment x exposure interaction, and letters denote Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted contrasts (Tables 4-5).

**Figure S5. Novel tank movement time (BT1).**
Total movement time in BT1 (n = 136) by concentration and exposure. Analysed with aligned-rank-transform ANOVA; the endpoint showed a treatment x exposure interaction, and letters denote Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted contrasts (Tables 4-5).

**Figure S6. Resistance attempts (BT1).**
Number of reinsertion attempts in BT1 (n = 136) by concentration and exposure. Analysed with a Poisson generalised linear model; no term was significant (Table 4).

**Figure S7. Condition factor (BT1).**
Fulton's condition factor (K = 100 x body mass [g] / standard length [cm]^3) in BT1 (n = 136) by concentration and exposure, shown descriptively. Condition factor was recorded as a control measure and was not modelled as an ethanol endpoint; no inferential test is reported.

## Table-only result (no dedicated figure)
